# Process Narrative: Wealth Management Client Onboarding

This document explains how a registered investment adviser (RIA) or broker-dealer interacts with a prospective client from the first point of contact through a fully funded and managed account. The process includes seven steps across four main actor groups: the prospect or client, advisor, compliance and operations, and portfolio management. The main regulatory requirements connected to each step are also noted throughout the process.

## Step 1: Prospect Inquiry and Discovery Call

**Owner:** Advisor and Prospect/Client

The advisor holds a discovery conversation to understand the prospect's asset level, financial goals, preferred type of relationship, desired services, and general fit with the firm. At this point, the conversation is usually exploratory and should avoid client-specific investment recommendations until the required disclosures and client information are in place. SEC Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI) applies to broker-dealers when a recommendation is made to a retail customer, while an RIA's fiduciary duty applies within the scope of its advisory relationship.

## Step 2: Know Your Customer (KYC) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Identity Verification

**Owner:** Compliance and Operations

Operations collects client identity information and performs risk screenings through the firm's Customer Identification Program (CIP). Key information may include:

- Government-issued identification
- Address and Social Security number
- Source of funds
- Tax forms, such as Form W-9 or Form W-8BEN

The information is checked against Politically Exposed Person (PEP), Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC), and adverse media screening sources. If a potential concern is identified, the firm may complete additional Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) before approving the client. These requirements and controls are supported by the Bank Secrecy Act, USA PATRIOT Act, applicable FinCEN rules, FINRA Rule 2090, and FINRA Rule 4512.

## Step 3: Suitability and Risk Tolerance Assessment

**Owner:** Advisor and Client

The advisor collects information needed to understand the client's investment profile. This includes risk tolerance, ranging from conservative to aggressive, time horizon, financial goals, liquidity needs, investment experience, and other relevant circumstances. The purpose is to make recommendations that fit the client's needs and overall financial situation.

Reg BI is the federal standard governing broker-dealers when making recommendations to retail customers. FINRA Rule 2111 applies to recommendations that remain within the rule's scope and requires a reasonable basis for believing that a recommendation is suitable based on the customer's investment profile. An RIA is subject to fiduciary duty under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 and must provide advice in the client's best interest.

## Step 4: Account Setup and Documentation

**Owner:** Operations

Operations collects and validates the completed account-opening package. This may include account registration, customer information, agreements, disclosures, beneficiary details, authorizations, and required custodian documentation. The information is checked against the client's KYC information and investment profile before being submitted to the custodian or clearing firm, such as Fidelity, Schwab, or Pershing.

Incomplete or inconsistent applications are returned for correction through the not-in-good-order (NIGO) process. NIGO items can delay account creation and create additional work for the advisor, client, and operations team. FINRA Rule 4512 establishes the customer account information that must be maintained, while FINRA Rule 3110 requires broker-dealers to maintain supervisory systems and written procedures for their activities and associated persons.

## Step 5: Investment Policy Statement (IPS) and Portfolio Mandate

**Owner:** Advisor and Client

The advisor translates the client's documented investment profile into a formal investment mandate, which is often captured in an Investment Policy Statement (IPS). Depending on the relationship, the IPS may include:

- Investment and return objectives
- Time horizon and risk tolerance
- Liquidity needs and tax considerations
- Asset allocation targets
- Investment restrictions and concentration limits
- Environmental, social, and governance (ESG) or socially responsible investing (SRI) preferences

The advisor and client agree on the mandate, which provides a framework for selecting, implementing, and monitoring the appropriate portfolio or model strategy.

## Step 6: Portfolio Construction, Funding, and Investment Implementation

**Owner:** Advisor and Portfolio Management

The advisor or portfolio management team translates the approved IPS and investment profile into an initial portfolio or approved model strategy. The client funds the account through cash or in-kind asset transfers, and authorized personnel execute the initial trades or apply the selected model.

The portfolio is established according to the client's documented objectives, risk tolerance, time horizon, liquidity needs, and investment restrictions. Benchmarks and monitoring parameters are also established to support ongoing performance and compliance reviews. Depending on the type of firm and recommendation, Reg BI, applicable FINRA suitability requirements, and the fiduciary duty under the Investment Advisers Act may apply at this stage.

## Step 7: Welcome and Ongoing Service Setup

**Owner:** Client Success and Operations

Once the account is funded and operational, the firm sends an onboarding confirmation, establishes the client's reporting and communication schedule, finalizes the CRM record, delivers required account or privacy notices, and schedules the initial review. The account then moves into the firm's ongoing servicing, monitoring, recordkeeping, and client information update processes.

Ongoing service remains subject to Regulation S-P for protecting customer information and records, along with applicable RIA fiduciary duties, broker-dealer and FINRA obligations, and books-and-records requirements throughout the client relationship.
